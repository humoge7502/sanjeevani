 The Red Sea crisis is the defining case of the decade. From late 2023, attacks forced median container ships
 onto the Cape of Good Hope, adding 10-14 days of transit; Red Sea transits fell by up to 90%, and the
 benchmark container freight rate hit $5,481 per FEU in the week of 23 January 2024 - a 417% spike against
 baseline. ITF-OECD put the pure re-routing cost at roughly $300 per 40ft container before insurance
 premiums, and UNCTAD documented the resulting effective capacity loss through 2024. What failed was
 not visibility - the world watched the attacks in real time. What failed was decision speed: enterprises
 re-planned manually across weeks, absorbing inventory bloat and service failures that better scenario
 automation could have compressed.

 Tariff shock is the second structural case. The US average effective tariff rate reached 16.8% by November
 2025 (z2data), and the de minimis exemption - duty-free entry for packages under $800 - ended for all
 countries on 29 August 2025, forcing every e-commerce and DTC flow through formal customs. McKinsey
 reported that tariff changes now affect 30% of global supply chain activities, and the US National Foreign
 Trade Council found 50% of surveyed firms had already rerouted shipments or changed suppliers in
 response. KPMG's May 2025 C-suite survey (300 executives) recorded 77% adapting. Columbus
 Consulting's verdict - tariff volatility will persist for years, not months - is precisely the operating assumption
 a resilience product should encode.

 The remaining cases complete the pattern. The Panama Canal drought cut transits to historic lows before
 recovering only by mid-2025. The October 2024 US East and Gulf Coast port strike lasted three days but
 produced a backlog that Xeneta said would take weeks to clear - because recovery sequencing, not
 awareness, is the bottleneck. The 2021 semiconductor shortage cost the auto industry $110-210bn in one
 year and cut global auto production by 26% in nine months. And COVID-era pharma dependency remains
 live: India sources roughly 70% of APIs (and 70-90% of key starting materials) from China, so a single
 regional shutdown propagates directly into the world's medicine cabinet.

 2.3 The pattern: sensing is solved, deciding is not
 Across all six cases the anatomy is identical. First, a sudden capacity, route, or regulatory shock. Second,
 weeks-to-months of manual re-planning (Cape re-routing, port backlog clearance, canal recovery,
 tariff-induced re-sourcing). Third, cost blowouts and human consequences - missed medicines, higher food
 prices, wiped-out farmer incomes, the exact harms the official theme text names. Fourth, and most important
 for this hackfest: the information needed to react well was usually available early. JPMorgan and project44
 both published Red Sea analyses in early February 2024, days before most corporate responses matured. The
 failure was never the absence of a signal; it was the absence of an organized decision machine between the
 signal and the SAP system of record.

 Disruptions propagate faster than humans can coordinate. The delta between alert-time and recovery-time is
 where supply chains bleed - and where an agentic system earns its keep.

 Implication for project selection Do not build another sensing dashboard. The winning wedge is the closed
 loop after the alert: verify, simulate, optimize, approve, execute, learn. That wedge is also, per Chapter 3, the
 one no funded player owns - and per Chapter 4, the one no academic group has evaluated end-to-end.

 the figure, and a parallel 2025 analysis puts annual vaccine wastage from temperature failures alone at
 roughly one in four doses. Beyond immunization, about twenty percent of all temperature-sensitive
 pharmaceuticals are damaged by or at least exposed to significant temperature excursions during transport,

 2.5 Where excursions actually happen
 The newest and most strategically useful evidence is about where excursions actually happen. A 2026
 practitioner study by Kanwal and a wave of 2026 trade coverage from air-cargo specialists converge on the
 same conclusion: in pharmaceutical air freight, excursions occur most frequently during ground operations -
 airport dwell, tarmac exposure, customs holds and the handoffs between truck and plane - not in flight.
 Cooperative logistics guidance explicitly warns against assuming temperature-controlled containers eliminate
 ground risk and against underestimating inspection dwell times. For a hackathon team this is gold: the failure
 window we simulate is narrow, nameable, frequent, and - judging by the trade press - still managed by phone
 calls and spreadsheets.

 3. The Self-Healing Thesis: Monitoring Is
 Saturated, Recovery Is Empty
 The thesis needs restating precisely, because the words are now fashionable. A monitoring system senses
 and alerts: it tells a human that a pallet is drifting toward its temperature limit. A self-healing system closes
 the loop: it predicts the breach, simulates recovery options against cost, risk and compliance constraints,
 executes the selected play through carrier and warehouse systems, writes the regulatory audit trail, and learns
 from every event. The six verbs - sense, predict, simulate, act, comply, learn - are the entire architecture.
 Monitoring is saturated because sensors are cheap and every vendor ships them; recovery is empty because
 acting autonomously requires the harder combination of simulation, integration and auditability that
 dashboards never needed.

 The language of self-healing has nevertheless gone mainstream, which is why the timing evidence matters.
 IBM’s consulting arm published its playbook for agentic supply chain operating models in April 2025; the
 SAP-connected sensing firm Sensos argued in June 2025 that AI agents fused with IoT sensing produce
 self-healing networks; boutique consultancies and Locus.sh spent 2025 and 2026 explaining that agentic AI
 moves logistics from decision support to execution, with Locus declaring 2026 the year agentic AI proves
 real return on investment. Most tellingly, SAP’s own July 2026 article quotes its supply chain thought leader
 foreseeing that "the future of supply chain will be self-healing, automated and driven by AI," with humans

 6. The Research Frontier: Solved, Open and
 Claimable
 The architecture is not just buildable; it is citable. Three research streams have converged on the self-healing
 cold chain without yet meeting in the middle. The first is agentic large-language-model operations: Jannelli’s
 2026 study on agentic LLMs in the supply chain - already cited nearly ninety times - introduces
 supply-chain-specific consensus-seeking mechanisms for autonomous agent coordination, and a companion
 2026 arXiv paper (Flowr) shows LLM agents scaling retail supply chain operations beyond simple
 request-response patterns. The second stream optimizes vaccine cold chains with learning: VacSIM, an
 Indian-led reinforcement-learning platform for COVID-19 vaccine distribution, demonstrated preventable
 cases on the order of nine thousand within forty-five days; VacciNet explored RL-based pandemic
 distribution; and Hu and colleagues (2025) apply deep reinforcement learning to cold-chain routing with
 multiple temperature conditions such as 2-8 degree and 0-4 degree shipments in the same network. The third
 stream supplies the sensing-and-prediction substrate: Konovalenko and Ludwig’s machine-learning study of
 temperature monitoring in pharmaceutical supply chains, Mangini and colleagues’ machine-learned risk
 scores for thermal mapping, and the 2026 Kanwal study pinpointing the ground-transfer dwell window.

 8. The Regulatory Stack: Compliance as
 Architecture
 Cold chain regulation is usually pitched as a burden; for Sanjivani it is the moat. The European Union’s Good
 Distribution Practice guidelines (2013/C 343/01) require a quality system covering procurement, storage and
 distribution, controlled storage areas, temperature control during transport through a dedicated annex, and
 corrective and preventive action when deviations occur - with a parallel guideline for active pharmaceutical
 ingredients (2015/C 95/01). The WHO’s TRS 961 Annex 9 provides the model guidance for storage and
 transport of time- and temperature-sensitive products, and the USP chapters complete the technical stack:
 <1079> for good storage and distribution practices, the newer <1079.4> for temperature mapping
 qualification of storage areas (effective 2022), and <1118> for monitoring devices. These chapters even
 define the ordinary world - controlled room temperature of 20-25 degrees Celsius with allowable short
 excursions between 15 and 30 - which is precisely the kind of bound an agent can be programmed against.

 Electronic recordality is the second pillar. The US FDA’s 21 CFR Part 11 defines when electronic records
 and signatures are trustworthy, reliable and equivalent to paper - audit trails, attribution, validated systems -
 and vendors such as TrueCold now market Part 11-validated cold chain records as a product category, proof
 that compliance sells. In India, the regulatory ground is moving toward us: CDSCO issued its first dedicated
 Good Distribution Practices guidelines for pharmaceutical products in April 2024 (document
 CDSCO/GDP.PP Ver. 00), with draft GDP guidance discussed through 2024 and comprehensive quality
 guidance continuing into 2025. A student product that generates GDP-grade audit trails automatically is
 therefore aligned not only with global doctrine but with a national regulator actively formalizing distribution
 quality - a coincidence of timing worth announcing on a slide.

 14. System Architecture: The Closed Loop
 The architecture’s spine is five cooperating agents. The Sense Agent ingests simulated IoT telemetry, lane
 and weather feeds and ground-handling events; the Predict Agent turns telemetry into an excursion-risk
 horizon using zero-shot time-series models; the Scenario Agent runs a lane digital twin that ranks recovery
 options by cost, risk and compliance; the Act Agent executes the approved play through carrier, warehouse
 and reefer interfaces; and the Comply Agent writes the GDP-grade audit trail and CAPA-ready excursion
 dossier. A human-in-the-loop approval console - rendered in Build Apps with Joule-style assistance - gates
 only the consequential actions, and a closed learning loop writes every outcome back into lane risk priors.
 The sections that follow present the same loop at three levels of resolution: the ten-stage autonomy loop, the
 seven-layer network blueprint, and the lane-level cold-chain view that the demo actually runs.

 The architecture has seven bands. The signal layer is always-on: news and geopolitical screening, weather and
 climate alerts, port and logistics status, tariff and trade-policy notices, and cold-chain IoT telemetry - all free
 or free-tier sources standing in for the Everstream/Prewave partner pattern. The data layer is SAP HANA
 Cloud free tier: events embedded in the vector engine, the supplier-plant-lane-customer network in the
 knowledge graph engine, and everything in a provenance-tagged store. The agent layer is a LangGraph state
 machine running six specialized agents. The simulation band (SimPy digital twin) and optimization band
 (Pyomo+HiGHS MILP, OR-Tools routing) are deterministic engines the agents orchestrate - the LLM
 reasons, the math decides. The governance gate validates every plan against policy and escalates by risk tier
 to a human. The SAP execution band posts approved actions through API-faithful surfaces, and the audit
 ledger records event, reasoning, scenario, recommendation, approval, action and outcome - the full decision
 trail the Learning agent replays to recalibrate thresholds.

 14.2 Safety, governance, and "what happens when the AI is wrong"
      Approval tiers: cost-banded actions (< $5k impact: auto-execute; $5k-50k: planner approval; > $50k or

 compliance-sensitive: COO approval). Thresholds live in the policy engine, editable per customer.

      Policy engine: every tool-call an agent makes toward a solver or an API is validated against parameter

 bounds, budget caps, sanctions and trade-policy constraints before execution. This is the guardrailed
 solver-calling pattern (patent hypothesis H4).

      Prompt-injection and data hygiene: external text (news) is treated as untrusted: summarization

 happens in a sandboxed pass; no external text reaches tool-parameters; every parameter is schema-validated
 before a solver call.




SAP Hackfest 2026 - Sanjivani Unified Edition                                                                                     42
SANJEEVANI - SAP Hackfest 2026 Unified Dossier: Strategy, Evidence and Execution




      Failure containment: agents are stateless between runs (state in checkpoints); any agent failure

 degrades the loop to advisory mode - the system recommends, humans execute; the SAP systems of record
 are never blocked.

      Rollback: execution posts are transactional with compensating actions defined up-front (e.g., a

 re-booking can be re-booked back); the audit ledger supports replay of any decision for post-mortem.

 The design answer to "what happens when the AI is wrong": the same thing that happens when a planner is
 wrong - a documented decision, reviewed by a human, bounded by policy, reversible by design. The
 difference is speed and memory.

 15. Agent Specifications and the Full Roster

  A1 - Disruption Sensing Agent

  Objective                       Detect candidate disruption events early with calibrated confidence.

  Inputs                          News/geopolitical feeds; weather alerts; port status; tariff notices; GSCPI macro index; IoT
                                  telemetry.

  Outputs                         Candidate events (type, location, magnitude, confidence, provenance) to the event bus.

  Tools                           Feed readers; embedding model; NLP classifier; clustering.

  Memory                          Rolling 30-day signal history; source-reliability priors.

  Decision logic                  Threshold score with source cross-check: signals agreeing across 2+ source classes rise;
                                  single-source stay candidates.

  Failure modes                   False positives (hoaxes, stale stories); missed slow-burn events; feed outages.

  Permissions                     Read-only on all data; can create candidate events only.

  Escalation                      Confidence > 0.6 hands to A2 for verification; below stays in watchlist.



  A2 - Event Verification & Scenario Agent

  Objective                       Debounce false positives and convert verified events into parameterized scenarios.

  Inputs                          Candidate events from A1; historical analog library; verifier prompts with cross-source
                                  retrieval.

  Outputs                         Verified events with confidence; 3/10/30-day scenarios (magnitude, duration, affected

 16.2 Data flows and storage decisions
 Three stores, three jobs. The relational core (HANA Cloud or PostgreSQL in dev) holds master data and
 open orders with typed schemas - the system of record the optimizers trust. The vector store (HANA vector
 engine) holds embedded event summaries and document chunks for semantic retrieval - this is what makes




SAP Hackfest 2026 - Sanjivani Unified Edition                                                                                          50
SANJEEVANI - SAP Hackfest 2026 Unified Dossier: Strategy, Evidence and Execution




 sensing feel intelligent rather than keyword-y. The graph store (HANA knowledge graph engine; NetworkX
 in dev) holds the supply network and the runtime DisruptionEvent nodes, enabling the propagation queries
 that answer "what does this event actually touch?" The event bus between agents is a lightweight queue
 (Redis Streams in dev; SAP Event Mesh shapes in the Layer-3 story). Every record carries provenance
 (source URL or system id), freshness (timestamp), and confidence (where computed) - the three metadata
 fields that make enterprise buyers take an AI system seriously.

 Round-1-friendly dataset plan Seed the MVP with a curated synthetic dataset: 4 suppliers (2 in a
 "China-like" cluster), 2 plants, 3 warehouses, 12 SKUs (2 biologics), 6 lanes (1 Red Sea-exposed), 30 days of
 demand, and 12 scripted disruption scenarios borrowed from the case studies. Everything in the demo is
 reproducible offline - no API outage can kill the finale.

 24. Research Roadmap: RecoveryBench-12
 24.1 Research question and hypothesis
 Research question: can a governed multi-agent LLM system - sensing, scenario generation, simulation,
 solver-orchestrated re-planning and human approval - recover disrupted pharmaceutical supply chains
 measurably better than rule-based planning and single-agent baselines, without violating policy constraints?

 Hypothesis H-R1: the multi-agent-plus-twin-plus-governance configuration reduces recovery cost and
 service-level loss versus all baselines across a 12-scenario benchmark, while producing zero policy violations

      and the ablation will show that the digital twin and the governance gate each contribute measurably

 (removing the twin degrades cost efficiency; removing the gate increases constraint violations). A secondary
 hypothesis H-R2: weak-signal scenario parameters generated with retrieval-grounded LLMs outperform
 naive heuristic scenarios on recovery outcomes at equal solver budgets.


 24.2 RecoveryBench-12: the benchmark contribution
 The field has a monitoring benchmark (January 2026) and no recovery-decision benchmark. We contribute
 RecoveryBench-12: twelve replayable disruption scenarios on a fixed synthetic pharma network, each with a
 seeded ground-truth recovery envelope (feasible cost floor, minimum service level, policy constraints) and a
 scoring harness that computes recovery cost, time-to-recover, fill rate, policy violations and decision-trace
 completeness for any system that conforms to a simple event-plan-outcome interface. Scenarios derive from
 the Chapter 2 case families (maritime closure, tariff shock, supplier failure, cold-chain excursion, port
 strike, demand surge, and combinations), with parameters perturbed to prevent overfitting. The dataset,
 harness and baselines are released openly - the absence of exactly this artifact is our citation-backed gap
 (Chapter 4, Gap 2), and releasing it maximizes adoption.

 27.1 Customer ROI equation (the value model that prices the product)
 The customer's annual value pool, with the equations we defend in Q&A: V = V_stockout + V_expedite +
 V_excursion + V_inventory + V_labor. V_stockout = (P_incidents x L_days x daily_margin_at_risk x
 reduction_rate); V_expedite = (expedited_spend x reduction_rate, benchmark 15-25%); V_excursion =
 (excursion_writeoffs x prevention_rate, benchmark 30-60% with governed response); V_inventory =
 (buffer_inventory_value x carrying_cost_rate x optimization_gain, benchmark 5-10%); V_labor =
 (planner_hours_per_event x events x hourly_cost x automation_rate, benchmark 70-90%). Worked example
 for a $180m exporter (illustrative ASSUMPTIONS): 4 major incidents/yr x 6 days x $220k daily
 margin-at-risk x 35% reduction = $1.85m; expedite reduction on $2.4m spend x 20% = $0.48m; excursion
 write-offs $0.6m x 45% = $0.27m; buffer-carrying savings $0.19m; labor savings $0.09m - a value pool near
 $2.9m against a $30k-60k price. Even at one-tenth attribution discipline, the ROI case clears. Figure 11
